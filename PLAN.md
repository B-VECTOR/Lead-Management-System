# LMS — Gap Analysis & Implementation Plan

> **RECONCILED against PRD v4.0 + Tech Req v17.0 on 2026-07-26.** These two documents supersede the PRD v3 / Tech Req v16 that the appendix (below the `APPENDIX A` divider) was built against. The v4.0/v17.0 change is **structural, not incremental**: the workflow goes 17 → **28 tasks** (single BD → Extension → Mining flow); **Stage** becomes a first-class tracked entity with **parallel** Mining ∥ Extension; **Country is dropped** (Domain **stays single-select** per decision D2 — a deliberate deviation from the spec's multi-select); new lead fields `flow_of_tasks` / `type_of_project`, `lead_type` gains **Extension**; **Finance goes live** with three payment gates that can **re-open** a closed task; **resource allocation is redesigned append-only**; **Project ID** is redesigned to a stable base + derived stage suffix; and **Hybernation / Short Closed are removed** as statuses (short-close stays an action).
>

This section re-scopes the rebuild. **The generic JSON workflow engine, auth, users, reference tables, holds, follow-ups, attachments, activity, notifications, and the append-only `project_details` history are largely reusable** — the rebuild is concentrated in the schema, the 28-task workflow JSON, the stage/finance/mining/allocation side-effects, and the matching UI.

### Phase R21 — Reassign is the lead custodian's right, not the assignee's — ✅ COMPLETE 2026-08-05

From the user 2026-08-05: *"The one who created the lead can reassign the task but once reassigned to someone else … the assigned person has the power to reassign this task, which should not happen. The one who created has the power all the time, whether to hold or reassign. In case of Execution Red is assigned, then he should also have the power to hold the task if assigned to him, but not reassign."*

**What's wrong today.** `permissions.can_reassign_task` is `return can_edit_task(user, task)` — literally the same actor set as editing ([permissions.py:128](backend/leads/permissions.py#L128)). So reassignment is a **transferable** right: the lead owner hands Task 5 to an Employee, and that Employee inherits the same power and can hand it on again, to anyone, without the lead's owner or creator being involved or even notified. Meanwhile the person who *does* own the lead loses both levers the moment they delegate: `can_reassign_task` and `can_hold_task` are keyed on `assigned_to`, so once the task is somebody else's, the creator/owner can neither pull it back nor pause it (only a Lead Admin can). The two rights are inverted — the one that should travel with the work (hold) is the only one shared with the lead's custodian, and the one that should stay with the lead (reassign) is the one that travels.

**The rule being implemented.** A task has two distinct rights:
- **Hold/unhold** — belongs to *whoever is doing the work* (the current assignee, Execution Red included) **and** to the lead's custodians. The person blocked is the person who knows the work is blocked.
- **Reassign** — belongs to the lead's **custodians only**, for the lead's whole life, no matter who the task is currently assigned to. Being handed a task confers no right to hand it on.

**Lead custodian** (new `permissions.is_lead_custodian`) = the **Lead Manager who created the lead**, the lead's **current owner** (`assigned_to`, whatever their role — the Default BD Person may be a plain Employee), or a **Lead Admin**. Same shape as `can_hold_lead` plus the any-role owner branch.

- [x] **R21-1 — `is_lead_custodian`.** One helper both task-level rights read, so "who stands behind this lead" is defined once and cannot drift between hold and reassign — [permissions.py](backend/leads/permissions.py).

- [x] **R21-3 — `can_hold_task` gains the custodian.** Assignee **or** custodian, so the creator/owner can pause a task they delegated, and the Red keeps hold on their own tasks as the user asked. **R21 decisions & deviations (2026-08-05):**
- **DD-R21-1 — "the one who created" is read as the lead's custodians, not `created_by` alone.** Taken literally, creator-only would strip the *owner* (`assigned_to`) of reassignment whenever the lead was created by someone else — and the Default BD Person is exactly who the workflow assigns tasks to and who delegates them in practice, so a literal reading would break the normal flow it is meant to protect.
